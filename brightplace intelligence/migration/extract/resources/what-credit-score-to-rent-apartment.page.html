What Credit Score Do You Need to Rent an Apartment?

Most landlords require a credit score between 620 and 700 to approve a rental application, though no universal minimum exists. The average renter credit score is 638 (as of Q3 2026). This guide covers what each score range means for approval, what landlords check beyond the number, and how to get approved with lower credit.

What Credit Score Do Most Landlords Require?

Most landlords look for a credit score of at least 620, though requirements vary by property type, location, and management company. A score in the 670-739 range puts renters in a strong position for approval at most properties. Here is what to expect at each tier.

Below 580 (Poor): Approval is difficult at corporate-managed properties. Expect requests for a guarantor or co-signer, a larger security deposit (often two to three months' rent), or proof of income well above the standard 3x rent threshold. Some renters at this level seek apartments with no credit check or work directly with private landlords.

580-619 (Fair): Many landlords will consider your application with conditions. You may need to provide additional documentation: pay stubs, bank statements, or a reference letter from a previous landlord confirming on-time rent payments.

620-669 (Good): This is the range where most standard apartments approve applications. Budget-friendly apartments average a renter score of 626, and mid-range properties average 638 (as of Q3 2026). You may still face a slightly higher deposit in competitive markets.

670-739 (Very Good): Strong approval odds. Landlords at this tier rarely require additional conditions. You are in the preferred range for most property management companies.

740+ (Excellent): Preferred-tenant status. Some properties offer reduced deposits or waived application fees for scores in this range.

What Do Landlords Actually See on Your Credit Report?

Landlords do not just see a single number. Most use a tenant screening company that generates a full report combining your credit history with rental-specific data. Understanding what they review helps you prepare.

Your FICO score is built from five components: payment history (35%), amounts owed and credit utilization (30%), length of credit history (15%), new credit inquiries (10%), and credit mix (10%). Landlords pay closest attention to payment history and utilization because these predict rent payment reliability.

The tenant screening report may also include prior eviction records, collections accounts, and public records like bankruptcies. A renter with an excellent FICO score can still receive a lower tenant screening score if the screening company flags other issues. One documented case showed a renter with a 788 FICO receiving a screening score of only 685 out of 1,000 due to factors beyond the credit score alone.

Before you apply, check your credit report for free at AnnualCreditReport.com. Reviewing your report first lets you catch errors and avoid wasting $30 to $75 (as of Q3 2026) in non-refundable application fees on properties where your score falls below their minimum.

Can You Rent an Apartment with a 500 Credit Score?

Renting with a credit score around 500 is possible, but expect additional requirements. Landlords approving renters at this level typically ask for a larger security deposit (two to three months' rent), a co-signer with a score of 680 or higher, or prepaid rent covering several months.

The cost difference is real. A renter with a 580 score applying for a $1,500/month apartment might pay $3,000 to $4,500 in security deposits (as of Q3 2026), compared to $1,500 for a renter with a 720 score. Over a 12-month lease, the renter with lower credit could pay $1,500 to $3,000 more upfront than someone with strong credit.

Private landlords tend to be more flexible than corporate property management companies. Before paying an application fee, ask the landlord or property manager directly about their minimum credit requirement. This single conversation can save you money and time. What Is a Guarantor and When Do You Need One?

A guarantor is a person who agrees to cover your rent if you cannot pay. Landlords typically require a guarantor when your credit score falls below 620, your income does not meet the 3x monthly rent threshold, or you have a thin credit file with limited history.

Guarantor requirements can be steep. In competitive markets, landlords may require the guarantor to earn 80 to 100 times the monthly rent annually and have a credit score above 700 (as of Q3 2026). For a $2,000/month apartment, that means the guarantor needs to show $160,000 to $200,000 in annual income.

If you do not have a personal guarantor, third-party guarantor services exist. These companies guarantee your lease for a fee, typically 2% to 6% of your annual rent (as of Q3 2026). On a $2,000/month apartment, that cost ranges from $480 to $1,440 per year. Learn more about how guarantors work in brightplace's guide to understanding guarantors on a lease.

How Can You Improve Your Credit Score Before Renting?

Start working on your credit at least three to six months before you plan to apply. The fastest improvements come from reducing credit card balances and correcting report errors.

Pay down credit card balances. Lowering your credit utilization below 30% is the single fastest way to raise your score. Some renters report a 40-point increase within one billing cycle after paying down balances. Make these payments at least 30 days before your application so the updated balance reports to the bureaus.

Dispute errors on your credit report. Pull your free report from AnnualCreditReport.com and review every account. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau estimates that one in five consumers has an error on at least one credit report. Disputing and removing an incorrect collection can raise your score significantly.

Use rent-reporting services. Services that report your on-time rent payments to credit bureaus can build your credit history while you rent. This is especially useful for renters with thin credit files.

Become an authorized user. A family member can add you as an authorized user on a credit card with a long, positive payment history. This can add years of positive history to your file.

What Are Your Rights If Your Rental Application Is Denied?

The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) protects renters who are denied housing based on credit information. Knowing these rights helps you respond effectively to a denial.

When a landlord denies your application based on a credit or screening report, they must provide an adverse action notice. This notice must name the screening company that generated the report, give you the company's contact information, and inform you of your right to request a free copy of the report within 60 days.

You have the right to dispute any errors on your tenant screening report. The screening company must investigate your dispute within 30 days and correct inaccurate information. The CFPB's tenant background check resources explain how to file a dispute.

Negative items on your credit report generally fall off after seven years. Bankruptcies may remain for up to 10 years. If a landlord uses outdated negative information beyond these timeframes, you can challenge the decision. Does Applying for an Apartment Hurt Your Credit Score?

Most apartment applications use a soft credit pull for initial screening, which does not affect your score. A hard pull, which may lower your score by 5 to 10 points temporarily, only happens when the landlord runs a formal credit inquiry through the bureaus.

If you apply to multiple apartments within a 14 to 45-day window, credit scoring models typically group those hard inquiries as a single event. This means shopping around for apartments during a focused search period will not compound the impact on your score.

Check whether the landlord uses a soft or hard pull before you apply. Ask the leasing office directly. This is standard information they should provide.

How Does a Low Credit Score Affect Your Security Deposit?

A lower credit score often translates directly into higher upfront costs. Many landlords charge additional security deposits for renters with credit scores below their standard threshold.

Consider this comparison for a $1,500/month apartment (as of Q3 2026):

Credit score 720+: Security deposit of $1,500 (one month's rent). Total upfront cost with first month's rent: $3,000.

Credit score 580-619: Security deposit of $3,000 to $4,500 (two to three months' rent). Total upfront cost with first month's rent: $4,500 to $6,000.

Credit score below 580: May require three months' deposit plus first and last month's rent. Total upfront cost: $6,000 to $7,500.

The difference between strong credit and poor credit can mean $3,000 to $4,500 more in upfront costs on a single apartment. Some states cap security deposits. California limits deposits to one month's rent for unfurnished units (as of Q3 2026). New York and New Jersey also have deposit cap laws. Check your state's rules before signing.

Frequently Asked Questions About Credit Scores and Renting

What is the minimum credit score to rent an apartment?

No universal minimum credit score exists for renting an apartment. Most landlords look for a score of at least 620, though requirements vary by property and market. Budget-friendly apartments may accept scores in the high 500s, while luxury properties often require 700 or higher. Ask the leasing office about their specific threshold before applying.

Can I lease with a 500 credit score?

Leasing with a 500 credit score is possible but requires extra steps. You may need a co-signer with a credit score of 680 or higher, a larger security deposit covering two to three months' rent, or proof of strong and steady income. Private landlords and smaller properties tend to be more flexible than large corporate management companies.

Do all apartments check credit scores?

Most apartments run some form of credit check during the application process. Large property management companies almost always check credit scores. Smaller private landlords may skip the credit check entirely or weigh other factors more heavily, such as income verification and references from previous landlords.

Can I raise my credit score 100 points in 30 days?

Raising your credit score by 100 points in 30 days is possible in limited situations, such as correcting a major error on your credit report or paying off a large credit card balance. For most renters, a realistic timeline is three to six months of consistent on-time payments and debt reduction to see a meaningful increase.

What credit score do I need to rent without a guarantor?

Renting without a guarantor typically requires a credit score of 650 to 670 or higher, combined with income that meets the landlord's threshold, usually three times the monthly rent. If your score falls below 650, most corporate-managed properties will ask for a co-signer or additional deposit.

Does a rental application show up on my credit report?

A rental application appears on your credit report only if the landlord runs a hard credit inquiry. Soft pulls, used by many landlords for initial screening, do not appear on your report and do not affect your score. Ask the leasing office which type of pull they use before submitting your application.

How far back do landlords look at credit history?

Most landlords review the past seven years of your credit history, which is the standard reporting window for late payments, collections, and other negative marks. Bankruptcies can remain on your report for up to 10 years. Recent positive payment patterns carry more weight than older negative marks.

What happens if I have no credit history at all?

Having no credit history (a "thin file") is different from having bad credit. Many landlords will work with applicants who have no credit if they can provide pay stubs, bank statements showing consistent savings, or a reference letter from a previous landlord. A co-signer or larger deposit may also satisfy the requirement.

Can I get a free copy of my credit report before applying?

Yes. AnnualCreditReport.com provides one free credit report from each of the three major bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) per year. Checking your own report is a soft pull and does not affect your score. Review your report before applying to catch errors and know your score.

What is the difference between a hard pull and a soft pull?

A soft pull previews your credit information without affecting your score. Landlords often use soft pulls for initial screening. A hard pull is a formal credit inquiry that appears on your report and may lower your score by 5 to 10 points temporarily. Multiple hard pulls within a 14 to 45-day window are typically grouped as a single inquiry.

Does paying rent build my credit score?

On-time rent payments do not automatically appear on your credit report. However, rent-reporting services can submit your payment history to the credit bureaus, which builds your credit file over time. This is especially helpful for renters with thin credit histories who need to establish a track record.

What percentage of income should go to rent?

The widely cited guideline is 30% of gross monthly income, though many renters in high-cost cities spend 40% or more. Landlords typically require income of at least three times the monthly rent. If your income is tight, learn more about what percentage of income should go to rent.
