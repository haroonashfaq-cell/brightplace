What Banyan Flats Is

Banyan Flats is a 289-unit apartment community at 2020 N Beckley Ave in Dallas's Oak Cliff neighborhood, opened in March 2024. Developed by Banyan Residential and managed by Bridge Property Management, it offers studio through three-bedroom apartments across five stories. The community includes both market-rate units and income-restricted units at 60% and 80% AMI tiers.

JHP served as the architect of record. The property sits adjacent to the Bishop Arts District, one of Dallas's most concentrated corridors of independent restaurants, coffee shops, and retail. For renters comparing apartments in the Banyan Flats area or the broader Oak Cliff market, this guide covers rent prices, amenities, location context, and the income restriction structure that most competing sources skip.

What the Income Restriction Means for Renters

Banyan Flats includes income-restricted units alongside market-rate apartments. No competing source explains what this means in practical terms.

AMI, or Area Median Income, is a benchmark published annually by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) used to determine income eligibility for restricted-rate housing programs. An income-restricted unit is a conventionally built apartment reserved for households whose income falls at or below a specified percentage of the local AMI. A market-rate unit carries no income eligibility requirement and is priced at whatever the current rental market supports.

At Banyan Flats, the "Essential" units are available at two AMI tiers: 60% AMI and 80% AMI. Income limits for qualification (as of Q3 2026):

1 person: up to $65,760/year

2 people: up to $75,120/year

3 people: up to $84,480/year

4 people: up to $93,840/year

Applicants must provide income documentation at the time of application. The income-restricted apartments at Banyan Flats are conventional units with the same amenities as market-rate homes. Eligibility is the only difference.

Banyan Flats Reviews: What Renters Report

Banyan Flats opened in March 2024, which means it has been operating for approximately two and a half years at the time of this review. New apartment communities in their initial lease-up and early operational phases are still calibrating management systems, maintenance response times, and community programming.

Renters evaluating Banyan Flats or any property this early in its lifecycle should weigh reviews from months 6 through 18 more heavily than initial reactions. Look for patterns in management response rather than individual complaints. Ask the leasing office directly about average maintenance ticket response times and how resident feedback is handled.

Specific questions worth asking: How many of the 289 units are currently occupied? Has on-site management been consistent since opening, or has there been turnover? What is the typical turnaround on a maintenance request? These questions give a clearer picture than aggregate ratings alone.
