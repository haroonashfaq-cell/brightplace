What Does $700 Rent Get You in Chicago? A Neighborhood-by-Neighborhood Breakdown

Cheap apartments in Chicago exist, but $700 per month sits well below the citywide median of $1,913 (as of Q3 2026). Only 13 to 23 units under $700 appear at any given time, and just 4% of Chicago rentals fall under $1,000. Here is what budget renters can realistically expect neighborhood by neighborhood.

How Much Is a 2 Bedroom Apartment in Chicago?

Chicago rent prices vary dramatically depending on which neighborhood you search. The citywide average for a one-bedroom apartment sits between $2,065 and $2,461 per month (as of Q3 2026), depending on the data source. Studios average around $1,666. Two-bedroom units run even higher, typically $2,500 or more in the city overall.

Those averages reflect the pull of high-rent neighborhoods near the Loop, Lincoln Park, and the Near North Side. Budget renters looking at the South Side and Far West Side find a completely different picture. A two-bedroom apartment for $700 is extremely rare in 2026, but individual units do appear in specific areas, particularly in older walk-up buildings or income-restricted housing. A more realistic budget for a two-bedroom in an affordable Chicago neighborhood is $900 to $1,400 per month (as of Q3 2026).

Rent in Chicago has increased roughly 35% since pre-pandemic levels. The city faces a shortage of approximately 120,000 affordable housing units, according to research from DePaul University's Institute for Housing Studies. That shortage makes every sub-$1,000 listing competitive.

Where Is the Cheapest Rent in Chicago?

The cheapest rent in Chicago concentrates on the South Side, Far Southeast Side, and portions of the West Side. Several neighborhoods consistently post median rents under $1,100 (as of Q3 2026). Here is what brightplace found when reviewing affordability by neighborhood, described by transit access, grocery proximity, and parks.

South Deering: Average rent around $600 per month (as of Q3 2026). Accessible via CTA bus routes and the Metra Electric District line at the 87th Street station. Calumet Park sits along the eastern edge, offering lakefront green space.

West Pullman: Average rent approximately $650 per month (as of Q3 2026). Metra Electric District service at the 119th Street station. Grocery access includes stores along Halsted Street. Fernwood Park provides recreation space.

Austin (West Side): Some of Chicago's lowest rents. CTA Green Line and Blue Line stations serve the area. Grocery options include shops along Chicago Avenue and Madison Street. Columbus Park, one of Chicago's largest parks at 135 acres, anchors the western boundary.

Englewood: Average rent $1,042 per month (as of Q3 2026). CTA Green Line stops at Halsted and Ashland. The neighborhood has seen recent infrastructure investment, including the Englewood Line trail project. Kennedy-King College provides a community anchor.

Grand Crossing: Average rent $1,035 per month (as of Q3 2026). CTA bus service connects to the Red Line. Proximity to Jackson Park and the planned Obama Presidential Center site.

Chatham: Median rent $1,185 per month (as of Q3 2026). CTA Red Line access at the 87th Street station. An established commercial corridor along 79th Street includes grocery stores, restaurants, and services.

Far Southeast Side: Average rent around $875 per month (as of Q3 2026). CTA bus routes and Metra connections. Proximity to Calumet River green space and industrial employment centers.

Calumet Heights: Average rent $953 per month (as of Q3 2026). CTA bus service and proximity to the Metra Electric line. Stony Island Park and the Calumet Park field house are nearby.

Avalon Park: Average rent $998 per month (as of Q3 2026). CTA bus connections to the Red Line. Avalon Park itself is a neighborhood anchor with athletic fields. Grocery options along Stony Island Avenue.

Burnside: Average rent $998 per month (as of Q3 2026). CTA bus routes serve the area. Close to the Metra Rock Island line at 91st Street. Small-scale commercial activity along Cottage Grove Avenue.

One pattern renters notice: on the CTA Blue Line, neighborhoods get cheaper the further north and west you travel. The area around Irving Park station is frequently mentioned as a more affordable option with direct downtown access.

What Does $700 a Month Actually Get You in Chicago?

A $700 monthly rent in Chicago typically means a studio or a small one-bedroom in a walk-up building on the South or West Side. Expect 400 to 600 square feet, radiator heat, coin-operated laundry in the basement, and limited or no in-unit amenities. Parking is almost never included.

The catch is that $700 in rent is not $700 in total housing cost. brightplace data shows the real monthly expense looks closer to this:

Rent: $700 per month (as of Q3 2026).

Gas and electric: $120 to $185 per month during winter. Polar vortex months can push gas bills above $300. Ask whether heat is included before signing; many older radiator-heated buildings include heat in rent.

CTA transit pass: $85 per month (as of Q3 2026). This replaces the cost of car ownership for many renters.

Renter's insurance: $20 to $30 per month.

True monthly cost: $925 to $1,000 in spring and summer, $1,000 to $1,100 or more in winter.

The single biggest lever on housing cost is splitting a two-bedroom apartment. A $1,930 two-bedroom split with a roommate costs $965 each (as of Q3 2026), saving roughly $9,700 per year compared to renting a solo one-bedroom. Are There Income-Restricted Apartments in Chicago Under $700?

Income-restricted housing is one of the most realistic paths to rent under $700 in Chicago. These programs cap your rent at a percentage of your gross income, typically 30%.

Section 8 (Housing Choice Voucher): A federal program administered by the Chicago Housing Authority (CHA) that subsidizes rent in privately owned apartments. Qualification requires earning below 50% of the Area Median Income (AMI). Section 8 is a federal rental assistance program where the government pays a portion of your rent directly to your landlord.

Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC): A federal tax incentive program that funds the construction of affordable apartment buildings. LIHTC properties set rents below market rate for tenants earning 30% to 60% of AMI. LIHTC stands for Low-Income Housing Tax Credit, the largest source of new affordable rental housing in the United States.

CHA public housing and mixed-income communities: The Chicago Housing Authority operates public housing and mixed-income developments across the city. CHA is the third-largest public housing authority in the nation, managing over 21,000 units and administering roughly 53,000 vouchers.

The city committed $300 million to 15 new affordable housing projects in 2026, with units targeting renters below 60% AMI. For federal rental assistance programs, visit the HUD rental assistance page.

Be honest with yourself about wait times. CHA voucher waitlists run months to years. Apply early, apply to multiple programs, and treat income-restricted housing as a long-term strategy rather than an immediate solution. What Should Budget Renters in Chicago Know Before Signing a Lease?

Chicago renters have unusually strong legal protections under the Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance (RLTO). The RLTO is a Chicago city ordinance that governs landlord-tenant relationships, including security deposit rules, required disclosures, and tenant rights to withhold rent for habitability violations.

Security deposits: Illinois requires landlords to return your deposit within 30 to 45 days. Chicago's RLTO adds that landlords must pay interest on security deposits annually. Get this in writing.

Heating: Before you sign, ask if heat is included. Buildings with steam radiators or boiler systems often bundle heat into rent. If heat is not included, budget $120 to $185 per month for gas during winter (as of Q3 2026). That seasonal spike catches many first-year Chicago renters off guard.

Parking: Budget $100 to $200 per month if you need a parking spot (as of Q3 2026). Going car-free with a $85/month CTA pass saves hundreds. The CTA rail and bus system covers most of the city.

Move-in costs: Expect first month's rent plus security deposit at minimum. Some buildings add a move-in fee of $100 to $300 on top of that. If you are renting your first apartment, plan for $1,400 to $2,000 in upfront costs at the $700 rent level.

Scam detection: Budget apartments attract scam listings. Since 2020, approximately 65,000 rental scams have been reported in Chicago, with losses totaling $65 million. Never wire money for an apartment you have not visited in person. Verify the landlord owns the property through the Cook County Assessor's website. If the price looks too good and the listing pressures you to act fast, walk away. The Illinois Attorney General's tenant rights page outlines your legal protections.

Free resources: Chicago Apartment Finders and Apartment People are free services for renters (landlords pay the fee). You can also call 311 for housing questions or contact Illinois Legal Aid Online for free legal guidance on lease disputes.

South Deering, West Pullman, and Austin consistently have Chicago's lowest rents. Average rents in these neighborhoods range from $600 to $950 per month (as of Q3 2026). Are there no-deposit apartments in Chicago?

Some Chicago properties offer move-in specials that waive or reduce security deposits. Illinois law caps deposits at 1.5 times monthly rent. Income-restricted apartments cap rent based on your earnings, typically at 30% of gross monthly income.
