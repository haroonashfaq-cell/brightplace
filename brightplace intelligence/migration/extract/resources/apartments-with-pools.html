Apartments with pools come in four types: outdoor seasonal, outdoor heated, indoor year-round, and rooftop. Pool access is almost always bundled into rent rather than charged separately, but communities with pools carry a rent premium of $70 to $118 per month (as of Q3 2026). Sun Belt states like Texas, Florida, and Arizona have the highest concentration. This guide breaks down pool types, real costs, seasonal availability by climate zone, pool rules, and specific communities in 10 states.

What Types of Pools Do Apartments Have?

Apartment pools fall into four categories, and the type you get depends on the building class and where you live. Most renters searching for apartments with pools will encounter outdoor seasonal pools. Indoor and rooftop options exist but are concentrated in luxury buildings and specific markets.

Outdoor seasonal: The most common type. Open May through September in most states, extending to October in the Sun Belt. Standard unheated pool with lounge chairs and a sundeck. Found across all apartment classes, including Class B and C communities.

Outdoor heated: Available year-round in mild climates like Florida, Arizona, and Southern California. In moderate climates, heating extends the swim season by four to eight weeks. Usually found at Class A communities because of the higher operating cost.

Indoor pool: Fully enclosed, climate-controlled, and open year-round regardless of weather. Most common in cold-climate cities like Chicago, Denver, and Minneapolis. Found almost exclusively at luxury high-rises and large-scale communities.

Rooftop pool: Elevated pool on the building rooftop or an upper-level deck, often with skyline views. Common in high-rise apartments in Dallas, Miami, Chicago, and Nashville. May be seasonal or heated depending on climate.

A quick note on marketing language: "resort-style pool" is a term you will see constantly. It typically means a lagoon-shaped or freeform pool with cabanas, outdoor grills, a fire pit, and sometimes a hot tub. The quality of resort-style pools varies enormously, so visit the pool area in person before signing.

Do Apartments with Pools Cost More?

Apartments with pools cost $70 to $118 per month more than comparable units without pools (as of Q3 2026), depending on the market. That premium is real, but it reflects the overall property class more than the pool itself.

Pool access is almost always bundled into rent. You will rarely see a separate line item for "pool access" on your lease. The reason pool communities cost more is straightforward: properties that invest in pools are typically newer, Class A or B+ buildings with higher construction costs, better finishes, and more amenities overall. The pool is one piece of a broader amenity package.

Some communities do charge a mandatory monthly amenity fee of $25 to $75 (as of Q3 2026) covering the pool, gym, and clubhouse. This fee appears as a separate line item and has no opt-out. If you see an amenity fee in your lease, ask what it covers and whether it applies year-round.

Here is the seasonal math that renters in colder climates should consider. If your rent premium for the pool is $100 per month and the pool is open five months out of the year, you are paying $1,200 annually for roughly 20 weekends of pool access. That works out to $60 per weekend visit if you only swim on Saturdays and Sundays. Swim three times a week during pool season, and the per-visit cost drops to around $20. The more you use it, the more the premium justifies itself.

What Is Pool Season at Apartments?

Pool season depends on your climate zone, and the difference between a year-round pool and a 12-week pool is the difference between a great deal and an expensive amenity you barely use. Here is how pool seasons break down across the country (as of Q3 2026).

Sun Belt year-round (FL, Southern CA, AZ, Southern TX): Pools open year-round. Some properties set December through February as "winter hours" with reduced staffing, but the pool stays accessible. Heated outdoor pools are common in this zone.

Sun Belt extended (Northern TX, GA, NC, TN): May through October, sometimes April if the pool is heated. This gives you roughly six months of swim season, making the rent premium easier to justify.

Moderate climate (CO, Northern CA, Mid-Atlantic): Memorial Day (late May) through Labor Day (early September). Roughly 14 weeks. Some heated pools stretch into late September.

Cold climate (IL, OH, MN, Northeast): Memorial Day through Labor Day only. Roughly 12 to 14 weeks. This is where the seasonal math problem hits hardest: 12 months of rent premium for three months of pool access.

Indoor pools: Year-round regardless of location. If year-round swimming matters to you and you live in a cold climate, an indoor pool eliminates the seasonal calculation entirely.

Heated outdoor pools can extend the season by four to eight weeks in moderate climates. Always ask the leasing office for exact opening and closing dates. Some properties close pools for maintenance in early spring, which can shorten your usable season by another two to three weeks.

What Are the Rules for Apartment Pools?

Standard apartment pool rules are more restrictive than most renters expect. Review these before signing your lease, not after.

Operating hours: Typically 8 AM to 10 PM. Some rooftop pools close at 9 PM due to local noise ordinances. Summer hours may be slightly longer than shoulder season hours.

Guest policy: Most communities allow one to two guests per resident. Some require guest registration 24 to 48 hours in advance through the front desk or a resident app. Guest access may be limited during peak summer weekends.

Glass containers: Prohibited at nearly all apartment pools. This is a safety and insurance requirement, not a suggestion.

Music and noise: Many properties prohibit portable speakers or enforce quiet hours in the pool area. Expect this rule to be inconsistently enforced.

Pool parties: Generally not allowed without advance management approval. Some luxury communities offer reservable cabana space or poolside event areas for a fee or deposit.

Children: Minors typically must be accompanied by an adult resident. Most apartment pools do not employ lifeguards, so children are your responsibility.

Pets: Almost universally prohibited in pool areas. If you are looking for pet-friendly amenities, some properties offer separate dog parks and pet wash stations instead.

Rules vary by property and are usually outlined in a pool addendum attached to your lease. Read it before you sign. The CFPB's renter resources page covers your rights as a tenant if amenity access issues arise.

Do Apartments Have Indoor Pools?

Yes, but indoor apartment pools are uncommon and concentrated in luxury high-rises in cold-climate cities. If year-round swimming is a priority, these are the markets where you will find the most options.

Chicago has the highest concentration of indoor apartment pools. Streeterville alone has eight or more buildings with indoor pool facilities. Wolf Point East offers an indoor and outdoor pool overlooking the Chicago River. Property 85 in the Loop includes an indoor pool, hot tub, steam room, and sauna. Expect 1BR rents starting at $1,900 to $2,100 per month in these buildings (as of Q3 2026). brightplace's Chicago pet owners guide covers more about renting in the city.

Denver is the other strong market for indoor pools. The Ventana offers a year-round indoor Olympic-size pool and hot tub. For orientation on Denver's rental market, 1BR units in buildings with indoor pools typically start at $1,500 per month (as of Q3 2026).

Minneapolis and New York City also have indoor pool options in select luxury towers, though they are less concentrated than Chicago's offerings.

Indoor pools almost always include additional amenities like hot tubs, steam rooms, and saunas. They also require significantly more maintenance and carry higher operating costs, which is why they are found predominantly in Class A communities.

What Should You Ask About the Pool During an Apartment Tour?

The listing photos will not tell you the full story. Visiting the pool area during a weekend afternoon in summer gives you the most accurate picture of what daily use actually looks like.

Here is what to ask the leasing office during your tour:

  • What are the exact pool hours and season opening and closing dates?
  • Is the pool heated? If outdoor, what temperature is it maintained at?
  • How many guests can I bring, and do I need to register them in advance?
  • Is there a separate amenity fee, or is pool access included in rent?
  • Are there cabanas, loungers, or shade structures? How many?
  • Does the pool area have grills, a fire pit, or an outdoor kitchen?
  • Is there a hot tub or spa?
  • When was the pool last resurfaced or renovated?
  • How many days was the pool closed for maintenance last year?
  • Are there security cameras or key-fob access to the pool area?

Frequently Asked Questions About Apartments with Pools

Do apartments with pools cost more?

Apartments with pools are typically $70 to $118 per month more expensive than comparable units without pools (as of Q3 2026). This premium reflects the overall property class rather than pool access alone. Pool access is bundled into rent at most communities. Some properties charge a separate monthly amenity fee of $25 to $75 covering all shared amenities.

What is pool season at apartments?

Pool season varies by climate zone. In Sun Belt states like Florida, Arizona, and Southern Texas, pools are open year-round. In moderate climates like Georgia and North Carolina, pools operate May through October. In cold climates like Chicago and Minnesota, pools are open Memorial Day through Labor Day, roughly 12 to 14 weeks per year.

Are apartment pools heated?

Some apartment pools are heated, but it depends on the market and building class. Heated outdoor pools are most common in Sun Belt states where they allow year-round swimming. In cold climates, indoor pools are heated by default. Outdoor heated pools at Class A communities in moderate climates extend the swim season by four to eight weeks. Ask the leasing office before signing.

What are the rules for apartment pools?

Standard apartment pool rules include operating hours of 8 AM to 10 PM, guest limits of one to two per resident, no glass containers, no pets, and a requirement that children be accompanied by an adult. Many properties prohibit portable speakers and require pool furniture to stay on the deck. Rules are in your lease addendum.

Do apartments have indoor pools?

Yes, though indoor pools are less common and concentrated in luxury high-rise communities in cold-climate cities like Chicago, Denver, and Minneapolis. Indoor pools are open year-round and often include hot tubs, steam rooms, and saunas. Expect higher rents at buildings with indoor pools, typically $1,800 to $3,500 per month for a 1BR (as of Q3 2026).

Can guests use the apartment pool?

Most apartment communities allow residents to bring one to two guests to the pool. Some properties require guest registration at the front desk or through a resident app 24 to 48 hours in advance. Guest access may be limited during peak summer weekends. Large gatherings or pool parties typically require advance approval from property management.

Who pays for apartment pool maintenance?

The property management company pays for pool maintenance, which costs $250 to $700 per month for apartment complexes (as of Q3 2026). This cost is factored into overall rents rather than charged separately. Pool maintenance includes water testing, chemical treatment, cleaning, equipment repairs, and compliance with local health department regulations.

Can you have a pool party at an apartment complex?

Most apartment complexes do not allow private pool parties without advance approval from management. Some luxury communities offer reservable cabana space or event areas near the pool for a fee or security deposit. Unapproved large gatherings may violate community rules and result in lease penalties. Contact your leasing office about event reservation policies.

What time do apartment pools close?

Most apartment pools close at 10 PM and open between 6 AM and 8 AM. Hours vary by property and season, with summer hours typically longer than shoulder season hours. Some luxury communities with rooftop pools enforce earlier closures at 9 PM due to local noise ordinances. Pool hours are stated in your lease addendum.

Are rooftop pools common at apartments?

Rooftop pools are increasingly common at luxury high-rise apartments in cities like Dallas, Miami, Chicago, and Nashville. They offer skyline views and premium lounge areas but carry higher rents due to the luxury positioning of buildings that include them. Rooftop pools are typically seasonal in non-Sun Belt cities and year-round in warm climates.

Do apartment pools have lifeguards?

Most apartment pools do not have lifeguards. Pool areas are typically designated as "swim at your own risk" zones with posted safety rules and emergency equipment on-site. Some larger luxury communities employ pool attendants during peak summer hours to monitor the area and verify resident access. Young children should always be supervised by an adult.

What is a resort-style pool at an apartment?

A resort-style pool is an apartment pool designed to resemble hotel or resort amenities. Features typically include a lagoon or freeform shape, a sundeck with lounge chairs, cabanas, outdoor grills, a fire pit, and sometimes a hot tub or splash pad. Resort-style pools are found at Class A and Class B+ apartment communities.
