What Is 113 University Place?

113 University Place is an 11-story commercial loft building at the southeast corner of University Place and East 13th Street in Greenwich Village, Manhattan. Built in 1902, the building contains approximately 57,739 square feet of office and commercial space, one block south of Union Square. For anyone researching this address for a prospective lease, a meeting, or neighborhood context, this profile covers the building's specifications, history, location, and practical details that broker listings leave out.

The building was designed by architect Frank Goodwillie and constructed by Isaac A. Hopper and Son for the Thirteenth Street Co. Its architectural style is neo-Renaissance, a common form for early 20th-century New York commercial buildings characterized by ornamental masonry, arched windows, and symmetrical facades drawing on Italian Renaissance forms. The building is historically associated with Local 1L of the Amalgamated Lithographers of America (ALA), which gives it one of its informal names in preservation records.

The History of 113 University Place

Architect Frank Goodwillie designed 113 University Place in 1902 for the Thirteenth Street Co., with Isaac A. Hopper and Son as the builder. The building is a neo-Renaissance loft, characteristic of the South of Union Square commercial corridor of that era. South of Union Square is a geographic submarket designation used by New York preservation and real estate professionals to describe the commercial corridor between 14th Street and Houston Street, west of Broadway.

Local 1L of the Amalgamated Lithographers of America (ALA) occupied space here, which gives the building one of its informal historic names: the Amalgamated Lithographers Building. The Village Preservation organization catalogs 113 University Place in its South of Union Square architectural survey. The building is not individually landmarked but sits on a historically significant block within the Greenwich Village preservation context.

What to Know If You Are Considering Space at 113 University Place

The building is Class B prewar, which carries specific tradeoffs. On the positive side: character, natural light, exposed ceilings, and strong bones. The corner exposure at University Place and 13th Street means three sides of windows on full-floor tenancies, a genuine differentiator for natural light. Direct elevator access to each floor is a practical advantage for small-to-mid teams.

Tenant-controlled HVAC means each floor sets its own temperature independently. The Union Square location provides excellent lunch options, strong commute access, and consistent foot traffic for businesses with client visits.

The honest tradeoff: University Place has consistent pedestrian and vehicle traffic. The building does not have a private lobby entrance in the style of a larger Class A property. Prewar Class B buildings require tenants who appreciate the loft format and are comfortable with older building systems. For many creative and professional firms, that tradeoff works well. For tenants who need a turnkey trophy build-out, newer Class A properties in Midtown may be a better fit.
