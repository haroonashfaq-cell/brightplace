## 3. CONTENT GAPS IDENTIFIED

### GAP 1: No Renter-Focused Empty Nester Guide for DC Suburbs (CRITICAL)
Every top-ranking article targets home BUYERS. Real estate agents dominate with content aimed at selling houses, condos, and townhomes. Nobody is speaking to the empty nester who wants to RENT an apartment -- whether temporarily to test a neighborhood, permanently to simplify finances, or strategically while deciding their next move. This is the single biggest gap.

### GAP 2: No Side-by-Side Neighborhood Comparison With Actual Rent Data
Existing articles mention neighborhoods (Old Town, Bethesda, Arlington) but never provide concrete rent comparisons. No article puts 1BR and 2BR rents for Bethesda, Arlington, Old Town Alexandria, and North Bethesda side by side with walkability scores, transit access, and healthcare proximity.

### GAP 3: No Maryland vs. Virginia Tax Analysis for Renters
Multiple articles compare MD vs. VA taxes, but always in a homeowner context (property tax focus). Nobody explains how state and county income taxes affect renters -- specifically, how a renter in Montgomery County MD pays ~$2,600/year MORE in income tax than an identical renter in Arlington VA at $100K income.

### GAP 4: No Transit/Walkability Analysis Tailored to Downsizers
Walk Scores and Metro station info exists across many sites, but nobody synthesizes it for the empty nester audience: which neighborhoods let you ditch a second car? Which areas have Metro + grocery + healthcare all within walking distance? The "can I go car-free or car-light?" question is completely unaddressed.

### GAP 5: No Healthcare Proximity Coverage
No competing article maps hospitals and medical centers relative to these neighborhoods. Bethesda sits next to NIH and Walter Reed. Arlington has VHC Health. Alexandria has Inova. This is a critical decision factor for 55+ downsizers that nobody covers.

### GAP 6: No Coverage of New Developments and Conversions
CityHouse Old Town (office-to-residential conversion, 199 units), Pike and Rose expansion, NoBE II affordable housing in North Bethesda -- these are all 2026 developments relevant to empty nesters, and no single article aggregates them.

### GAP 7: No "What to Expect" Lifestyle Content
Every article is transactional (here's where to buy/rent). Nobody covers the emotional and practical adjustment of going from a 3,000 sq ft house to a 1,100 sq ft apartment -- the storage reality, the neighbor proximity, the benefits of maintenance-free living.

### GAP 8: No Purple Line Impact Analysis
The Purple Line (Bethesda to New Carrollton, opening December 2027) will dramatically improve connectivity in Montgomery County suburbs. No downsizing guide mentions it or explains how it changes the calculus for neighborhood selection.

## 6. ADDITIONAL REFERENCE DATA

### Property Tax Rates (2026)

**Montgomery County, MD:**
- County rate: $0.9321 per $100 assessed value
- State rate: $0.112 per $100 assessed value
- Fire tax: $0.043-$0.098 per $100 (varies by district)
- Effective rate: ~1.19% (median)
- Example: $600K home = ~$7,500-$9,000/year ($625-$750/month)

**Arlington County, VA:**
- County rate: $1.053 per $100 assessed value (2026, 2-cent increase from 2025)
- Effective rate: ~0.90%
- Median tax bill: $7,774 on $864,800 median home value
- Personal property tax (vehicles): $5.00 per $100

**Relevance for article:** Property tax savings is a major financial motivator for downsizing. A homeowner selling a $1M house in Montgomery County eliminates ~$11,900/year in property taxes. Even paying $3,000/month rent ($36,000/year), the net housing cost change depends on their mortgage situation -- this is the kind of math no competitor article does.

---

### State Income Tax Comparison (Critical for MD vs. VA Decision)

**Virginia:**
- Top rate: 5.75% (on income above $17,001)
- No local/county income tax
- Social Security: Not taxed
- Retirement income deduction: Up to $12,000
- Military retirement: Fully exempt

**Maryland (Montgomery County):**
- State top rate: 6.5%
- Montgomery County local rate: 3.20%
- Combined top marginal rate: 9.70%
- At $100K income: MD charges ~$7,535 vs. VA charges ~$4,936
- **Annual savings in VA vs. MD: ~$2,600/year at $100K income**

**Relevance for article:** This is a massive differentiator that no renter-focused competitor mentions. An empty nester choosing between Bethesda (MD) and Arlington (VA) needs to know they could save $2,600+/year in income taxes on the VA side -- enough to offset a portion of rent difference.
