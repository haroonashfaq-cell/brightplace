## Notes for Writing Agent

1. **Affordability is the real topic.** The keyword says "average rent" but the PAA data proves renters want to know "can I afford it." Lead with data, immediately pivot to affordability. Every section should connect rent numbers back to income.

2. **Use median ($1,545) as the primary anchor number.** Average ($1,713) is mentioned for context but median is more useful and more defensible. Explain the distinction once, then use median throughout.

3. **Show your math.** Every salary-to-rent calculation should be shown step by step ($20/hr x 40 hrs x 52 weeks = $41,600/year). Renters don't trust unsourced "you need X salary" claims. Showing the work builds trust and is more extractable by AI engines.

4. **Date-stamp everything.** This article will be competing against data pages that update automatically. Our freshness advantage is Q3 2026 data. Every figure needs "(as of Q3 2026)" to prove it.

5. **No banned sources.** The SERP data references Apartments.com, Zillow, and RentHop. NEVER cite these in the article. Use data points without attribution to specific ILS platforms. Frame data as "based on national housing data" or "brightplace's analysis of rental market data."

6. **Do NOT use markdown tables for the city comparison or salary breakdown.** Use bold-label bullet point format only. Webflow strips tables.

7. **The 30% rule is the through-line.** It should appear in the salary section, both PAA affordability sections, the FAQ, and the opening. This is the entity we want to own.

8. **Honest tradeoffs matter.** Don't just say cheap cities are great. Note that Houston has no state income tax but limited public transit. Note that Phoenix is affordable but summers are extreme. One honest tradeoff per city-comparison bullet makes the content trustworthy.

9. **Entity density targets:** "1 bedroom apartment" 12-15x, "30% rule" 5-7x, "$1,545" 4-6x, "brightplace" 3-5x in body. City names 3-5x each for the major ones (NYC, Austin, Houston, Phoenix).

10. **The "true monthly cost" gap is our unique insight.** No competitor covers this. Make the worked example concrete and memorable: "$1,545 in rent becomes $1,780-$1,930 once you add utilities and fees." This is the kind of specific, practical data that gets cited by AI engines.
