### H2: What Does a Terrace Apartment Mean?
**Target:** 140-180 words
**Must cover:** Clear definitions of all three outdoor space types in bold-label format:
- **Terrace:** A flat, open outdoor area that sits on a solid structure (rooftop, setback, or ground-level platform). Terraces are typically larger than balconies, ranging from 100-500+ sq ft. They can accommodate outdoor furniture, dining sets, planters, and sometimes even grills. Common on penthouses, ground-floor units, and buildings with setbacks. A terrace does not protrude from the building; it rests on a flat surface.
- **Balcony:** A smaller, elevated platform that extends outward from the building facade, enclosed by a railing. Typical apartment balcony size: 25-80 sq ft. Found on mid-rise and high-rise apartments on upper floors. Usually fits 1-2 chairs and a small table. Provides views and fresh air but limited functional space.
- **Patio:** A ground-level outdoor area, typically paved or concrete, accessed directly from a ground-floor or garden-level unit. Common at garden-style apartment communities. Size: 50-200 sq ft. May include a small fenced area. Often the most affordable outdoor space option.
- **Juliet balcony:** A decorative railing on a window or door with no usable floor space. Not true outdoor space; provides ventilation and aesthetic only.

- **Cost:** Terraces command a higher rent premium than balconies due to larger usable space
- **Availability:** Balconies are far more common in apartment buildings. Terraces are rarer and found primarily in luxury or converted buildings.
