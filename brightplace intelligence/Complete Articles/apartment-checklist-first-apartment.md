# A Practical Checklist for Your First Apartment

*Last reviewed: July 2026*

## What You Need Before Move-In Day

An apartment checklist for first apartment renters covers five areas: bedroom, kitchen, bathroom, living room, and move-in logistics. Most first-time renters focus on buying items and miss the logistics that protect their money. Renters insurance alone costs $15-30/month (as of Q2 2026), and many landlords require it as a lease condition. This list is for first-time renters preparing to move in, organized by priority so you can tackle what matters first and add the rest over time.

## Before You Move In

The items you buy matter less than the logistics you handle before bringing anything into the apartment. Four things should be done at least one week before your move-in date.

Set up utilities (electric, gas, water, internet) so they are active on move-in day. Research internet providers before signing the lease; some buildings are locked into a single provider. Purchase **renters insurance**, a policy that covers your personal belongings inside the apartment against theft, fire, and certain water damage. It does not cover the building itself. Many landlords require proof of coverage before handing over keys. Budget $15-30/month (as of Q2 2026).

Read your lease for early termination clauses, guest policies, and pet rules. If anything is unclear, ask your landlord in writing before move-in day. For a full walkthrough, see brightplace's [guide to renting an apartment](https://www.brightplace.ai/guides/how-to-rent-an-apartment).

On a $1,800/month apartment, your upfront move-in costs (first month, last month, and security deposit) often total $3,600-$7,200 at signing (as of Q2 2026 market norms). A **security deposit** is money paid upfront to the landlord, typically equal to one month's rent, held against damage or unpaid rent and returned at move-out if the unit is left in good condition. For a breakdown of what renters actually pay beyond base rent, see [your true monthly cost](https://www.brightplace.ai/guides/your-true-monthly-cost).

### The Move-In Inspection

Complete a **move-in inspection** before you bring a single box inside. A move-in inspection is a documented walkthrough of the apartment, completed with photos before you bring any belongings in, that records existing damage so you are not held responsible for it at move-out. Photograph every wall, floor, appliance, and fixture. Note the date on each photo. Send the documented damage to your landlord in writing. This protects your security deposit when you move out.

## Bedroom Essentials

The mattress is the one item worth spending more on. You spend roughly a third of your life sleeping on it, and a poor mattress compounds over months in ways that a cheap lamp does not.

**Day 1 (needed the first night):**
- Mattress
- Bed frame (or mattress on the floor temporarily)
- Two sets of sheets
- Pillows
- Comforter or blanket
- Window covering for privacy (tension rod + curtain if none provided)

**Add Later (can wait 2-4 weeks):**
- Dresser
- Nightstand
- Lamp
- Full-length mirror
- Hangers
- Laundry hamper

## Kitchen Essentials

Equipping a functional kitchen from scratch costs roughly $150-400 new, or $50-150 if sourcing secondhand (as of Q2 2026). You do not need a fully stocked kitchen on day one. You need enough to make one meal and clean up after it.

**Day 1 (cook one meal, clean up):**
- One pot, one pan, one baking sheet
- Chef's knife and cutting board
- Plates and bowls for two
- Glasses and mugs
- Utensils (forks, knives, spoons)
- Can opener
- Dish soap, sponge
- Trash can and bags

**Add Later:**
- Blender
- Toaster
- Coffee maker
- Food storage containers
- Measuring cups and spoons
- Dish towels

## Bathroom Essentials

The item most renters forget: shower curtain and hooks. Apartments almost always provide the rod but not the curtain. Without one, your first shower floods the floor.

- Shower curtain, liner, and hooks
- Bath mat
- Two bath towels, two hand towels
- Toilet paper (buy more than you think you need)
- Plunger (buy before you need it)
- Toilet brush
- Hand soap
- Toiletries (toothbrush, toothpaste, shampoo, body wash)
- Small trash can
- Over-toilet shelf if storage is limited

## Living Room Essentials

Living room furniture is the most expensive category and the easiest to phase in over time. Start with seating and lighting. Everything else can wait.

One critical step before buying any large furniture: measure your front door opening and any interior doorways. Standard sofa depths run 36-40 inches. Standard interior doorways are 32-36 inches. Check both before purchasing.

**Day 1:**
- Couch, loveseat, or futon (whatever fits through the door and the budget)
- Floor lamp (most apartments have inadequate overhead lighting)
- Extension cord and surge protector
- Curtains or blinds for privacy

**Add Later:**
- Coffee table
- TV stand
- Bookshelf or shelving unit
- Side tables
- Area rug

## Cleaning Supplies and Home Tools

You will want to clean the apartment before unpacking, and you cannot do that without supplies. Buy these before move-in day.

**Cleaning supplies:**
- All-purpose spray cleaner
- Paper towels
- Trash bags (multiple sizes)
- Dish soap
- Broom and dustpan or small vacuum

**Basic tool kit:**
- Phillips and flathead screwdrivers
- Hammer
- Measuring tape
- Command strips or adhesive hooks

**Command strips** are the renter-specific alternative to nails and screws. Many landlords prohibit wall damage, and holes in drywall can come out of your security deposit. Adhesive hooks and strips let you hang frames, towel hooks, and organizers without drilling.

## The First-Night Box

Pack a single labeled box or bag that stays with you during the move, not on the truck, not in the last load. This box contains everything you need for the first 18-24 hours so you do not have to dig through packed boxes after an exhausting move.

**First-night box contents:**
- Phone charger
- Toiletries
- One towel
- Change of clothes
- Toilet paper
- Paper towels
- Hand soap
- Basic snacks and water
- Any medications
- Lease documents and ID

The most common call from first-time renters the morning after move-in is about something they cannot find. The first-night box solves this.

## How to Furnish Your First Apartment on a Budget

Three specific strategies keep costs down without sacrificing function.

**Facebook Marketplace and local thrift stores** are the strongest sources for furniture. Sofas, dressers, desks, and shelving regularly sell at 40-80% below retail. Inspect upholstered items carefully before purchasing.

**Dollar stores** cover bathroom and cleaning basics at a fraction of grocery store prices. Trash cans, dish soap, sponges, hangers, and storage bins are functional purchases where brand does not matter.

**IKEA** offers the strongest price-to-function ratio for kitchen items, shelving, and storage solutions. Their kitchen starter sets and basic cookware cover the Day 1 list for under $75 (as of Q2 2026).

One more piece of advice: do not buy everything at once. Live in the apartment for two weeks before making major purchases. You will discover how you actually use the space, where you need storage, and how the light falls before committing to furniture placement and large items.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should go in a first apartment checklist printable version?

A printable first apartment checklist should be organized by room (bedroom, kitchen, bathroom, living room) with two columns: Day 1 essentials and items to add later. Include a separate section for move-in logistics (utilities, renters insurance, move-in inspection). This article is structured for that purpose; save or bookmark it for reference.

### How much does it cost to furnish a first apartment?

Budget $1,500-$4,000 for a functional first apartment setup if buying new, or $500-$1,500 if sourcing key pieces secondhand (as of Q2 2026). The mattress and sofa are the two largest expenses. Kitchen and bathroom basics total $200-$500 combined. Phase purchases over the first month rather than buying everything at once.

### What are the most commonly forgotten items for a first apartment?

Shower curtain and hooks, a plunger, trash bags in multiple sizes, a basic tool kit, and a toilet brush are the items most first-time renters forget. A first-night box (phone charger, toiletries, towel, change of clothes) is the most commonly skipped packing step and the one that causes the most frustration on move-in day.

### Do I need renters insurance for my first apartment?

Many landlords require renters insurance as a lease condition. Even when optional, it covers your personal belongings against theft, fire, and certain water damage at $15-30/month (as of Q2 2026). It does not cover the building. A single stolen laptop can cost more than a year of premiums. It is worth carrying.

### What should I buy first for my first apartment?

Buy the mattress, sheets, pillows, and a shower curtain before anything else. These are the items you need the first night. Kitchen basics (one pot, one pan, plates, utensils) come next. Living room furniture can wait days or weeks. Prioritize sleep, hygiene, and one functional meal.

### What is a move-in inspection and why does it matter?

A move-in inspection is a documented walkthrough of the apartment completed with photos before you bring any belongings in. Photograph walls, floors, appliances, and fixtures. Date the photos and send them to your landlord in writing. This documentation protects your security deposit by proving which damage existed before you moved in.

### What is a first-night box?

A first-night box is a single labeled bag or box kept accessible during the move containing everything needed for the first 24 hours: phone charger, toiletries, towel, change of clothes, toilet paper, snacks, medications, and lease documents. It prevents the most common move-in frustration of needing something buried in an unknown box.
