# How Long Does a Landlord Have to Return Your Security Deposit?

*Last reviewed: September 2026*

How long does a landlord have to return a security deposit? Most states give a landlord 14 to 30 days after you move out to return it or send an itemized statement of deductions. The exact deadline depends on your state, and missing it can cost the landlord two or three times the amount withheld.

That last part is what most renters never learn. The deadline is not a guideline. In several states it carries a statutory penalty, which changes the math on whether chasing your money is worth the effort.

## What Is the Security Deposit Return Deadline in Your State?

Deposit return deadlines run 14 to 30 days in the ten states verified below (as of Q3 2026). Six of the ten use 30 days, which makes it the most common deadline, but the outliers matter: New York gives a landlord just 14 days, and Arizona counts business days rather than calendar days.

Every figure below was read directly from the state's own statute or court self-help resource in September 2026.

| State | Deadline after move-out | Source |
|---|---|---|
| New York | 14 days | NY Gen. Oblig. Law §7-108 |
| Arizona | 14 business days | A.R.S. §33-1321 |
| Florida | 15 days, or 30 to notice a claim | Fla. Stat. §83.49 |
| California | 21 days | Cal. Civ. Code §1950.5 |
| Massachusetts | 30 days | M.G.L. c.186 §15B |
| Michigan | 30 days | Michigan Legal Help |
| North Carolina | 30 days, 60 if damage is unclear | N.C.G.S. §42-52 |
| Ohio | 30 days | Ohio Rev. Code §5321.16 |
| Texas | 30 days | Tex. Prop. Code §92.103 |
| Washington | 30 days | RCW 59.18.280 |

The detail under each state matters as much as the number, because the clock does not always start on the day you move out.

**New York, 14 days:** The landlord must provide an itemized statement and return the balance within 14 days of the tenant vacating. Deposits are capped at one month's rent. A willful violation carries punitive damages of up to twice the deposit. ([NY Gen. Oblig. Law §7-108](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-108))

**Arizona, 14 business days:** The statute excludes Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, so 14 business days is closer to three calendar weeks. The clock also requires tenant demand, not just move-out. Deposits are capped at one and a half months' rent, and wrongful withholding carries damages of twice the amount withheld. ([A.R.S. §33-1321](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/33/01321.htm))

**Florida, 15 or 30 days:** Florida splits the timeline. A landlord making no claim has 15 days to return the full deposit. A landlord who intends to keep part of it has 30 days to send written notice of that claim by certified mail. You then have 15 days to object in writing. ([Fla. Stat. §83.49](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/83.49))

**California, 21 days:** The landlord has 21 days to return the deposit, or return the balance with an itemized statement. Any deduction over $125 requires attached invoices or receipts. Bad-faith retention exposes the landlord to twice the deposit in damages, on top of returning it. ([California Courts self-help](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/guide-security-deposits-california))

**Texas, 30 days:** Thirty days after you surrender the premises. Bad faith is expensive here: three times the portion wrongfully withheld, plus fees. ([Tex. Prop. Code §92.103](https://guides.sll.texas.gov/landlord-tenant-law/security-deposits))

**Massachusetts, 30 days:** Thirty days after the tenancy ends, with an itemized list sworn under penalty of perjury and supporting evidence attached. Failure means three times the deposit, plus 5% interest and attorney's fees. ([M.G.L. c.186 §15B](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter186/Section15B))

**Washington, 30 days:** Thirty days after the rental agreement ends and you vacate. A court may award up to twice the deposit for an intentional refusal, plus costs and attorney's fees. ([RCW 59.18.280](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=59.18.280))

**Ohio, 30 days:** Thirty days after termination and delivery of possession. Damages equal the amount wrongfully withheld plus reasonable attorney's fees. ([Ohio Rev. Code §5321.16](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.16))

**North Carolina, 30 days:** Thirty days, with one exception worth knowing. If the full extent of damage cannot be determined in time, the landlord may give an interim accounting at 30 days and a final accounting within 60 days. ([N.C.G.S. §42-52](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_42/GS_42-52.html))

**Michigan, 30 days:** Thirty days, counted from when the landlord receives your forwarding address rather than from move-out. Deposits are capped at one and a half months' rent, and the penalty is twice the deposit. ([Michigan Legal Help](https://www.michiganlegalhelp.org/resources/housing/your-security-deposit-what-it-and-how-get-it-back))

If your state is not listed, check its current statute directly before acting. These rules change, and a page written last year may already be wrong.

## When Does the Security Deposit Clock Actually Start?

The clock rarely starts on the day your lease ends. Most statutes tie it to a later event: surrendering possession, the tenancy terminating, or the landlord receiving your forwarding address. Getting the start date wrong is the most common reason a renter thinks a landlord is late when the deadline has not yet run.

Three different triggers appear in the statutes above.

**Surrender of possession:** Texas runs its 30 days from the date you surrender the premises, meaning keys returned and the unit vacated, not the calendar date your lease expired.

**Termination of tenancy plus vacating:** Washington and Ohio both require the agreement to have ended and the unit to be empty.

**Receipt of your forwarding address:** Michigan starts counting only once the landlord has your new address in writing.

If you moved out early, the date you handed back the keys is usually the one that counts. That also matters when [you break a lease](https://www.brightplace.ai/resources/what-happens-when-you-break-a-lease), where move-out and lease-end dates can be months apart, and on a [short-term lease](https://www.brightplace.ai/resources/short-term-lease-agreement) or a [month-to-month arrangement](https://www.brightplace.ai/resources/month-to-month-vs-12-month-lease), where the tenancy can end on a date that is not the end of a month.

## Why Does a Written Forwarding Address Decide So Many Cases?

Because in several states, no written address means no claim. Ohio is explicit: a tenant who fails to provide a written forwarding address "shall not be entitled to damages or attorneys fees." Texas law says a landlord is not obliged to refund or itemize until the tenant gives one. Michigan requires it within four days of moving out.

Renters lose winnable cases here constantly. They call the landlord, or text the new address, or assume the leasing office already has it because it is on a form somewhere. Verbal notice is not written notice.

Put your forwarding address in writing even in states that do not require it. It takes one email and it removes the landlord's easiest defence. Send it on the day you hand back the keys, and keep a copy.

## What Can a Landlord Legally Deduct From Your Security Deposit?

A landlord can deduct for unpaid rent and for damage beyond normal wear and tear. They cannot deduct for the ordinary ageing of a unit that you lived in and maintained. **Normal wear and tear** means the decline in condition that happens through ordinary use: faded paint, worn carpet traffic lanes, small nail holes.

The line sits where ordinary use ends and neglect or accident begins.

**Usually deductible:** unpaid rent, unpaid utilities, large holes in walls, pet damage, broken fixtures, stains and rips in carpet, removal of items you left behind.

**Usually not deductible:** faded or slightly scuffed paint, carpet worn along walking paths, minor nail holes from hanging pictures, loose grout, ordinary cleaning of a unit you left clean.

**Useful life** is the concept that settles most carpet and paint arguments. Carpet and paint depreciate on a schedule, and courts commonly apply that schedule when a landlord charges full replacement cost for something already part-way through its life. A landlord billing you the entire cost of a carpet that was years old when you moved in is asking you to pay for their asset, not your damage.

Several states also police the paperwork. California requires receipts or invoices for any deduction over $125. Massachusetts requires the itemized list to be sworn under penalty of perjury with written evidence attached. A bare list of charges with no documentation is often not enough.

This is the same category of money as a [pet deposit versus a pet fee](https://www.brightplace.ai/resources/pet-deposit-vs-pet-fee), where one is refundable and the other never was.

## What Happens If a Landlord Misses the Security Deposit Deadline?

Missing the deadline usually costs the landlord more than the deposit itself. Penalties across the verified states run one to three times the amount wrongfully withheld, and several add attorney's fees. In some states a landlord who blows the deadline forfeits the right to keep any of it, even for damage they could otherwise have charged.

**Three times the amount:** Texas, for bad-faith retention, plus fees. Massachusetts, for failing to return or itemize within 30 days, plus 5% interest and fees.

**Twice the amount:** New York for willful violation, California for bad-faith retention, Arizona for wrongful withholding, Michigan, and Washington for intentional refusal.

**The amount withheld plus fees:** Ohio.

The practical consequence is that a modest deposit can become a claim worth pursuing. An $800 deposit wrongfully withheld in Texas is a potential $2,400 claim. That is a different decision from chasing $800.

## How Do You Write a Demand Letter for a Security Deposit?

Send a written demand before filing anything. State the amount, the date you moved out, the date you provided your forwarding address, and the statutory deadline your state sets. Ask for the balance by a specific date. Send it certified mail with return receipt so you have proof it arrived.

This step resolves a surprising share of disputes on its own. A landlord who has been ignoring emails often pays in full once there is a paper trail that would be read aloud in court.

Keep it short and unemotional. Include your new address again. Attach your move-out photos if you have them. Give a reasonable deadline, usually 10 to 14 days, then follow through if it passes.

## Is It Worth Taking a Landlord to Small Claims Court?

Often yes, and more often than renters assume. Small claims court is designed for exactly this: filing fees are modest, you do not need a lawyer, and the penalty multipliers mean the amount at stake is frequently double or triple the deposit. Many landlords settle once served, before any hearing happens.

Check your state's small claims limit. Texas hears disputes under $20,000 in justice court without an attorney. Michigan's small claims limit is $7,000, with larger claims going to district court.

Bring your lease, your written forwarding address and proof of delivery, your move-out photographs, the itemized statement if you received one, and a copy of your demand letter. Judges in these cases want dates and documents, not argument.

## How Can You Protect Your Security Deposit Before You Move Out?

Document the unit at move-in and again at move-out. Timestamped photographs of every room, including floors, walls, appliances and any existing damage, are the single most useful thing you can produce later. Most disputes come down to whether a problem existed before you arrived, and photographs answer that in a way memory does not.

Do this in order.

**At move-in:** photograph everything before you unpack, note existing damage in writing, and send it to the landlord so it is on record. An [apartment checklist](https://www.brightplace.ai/resources/apartment-checklist-first-apartment) covers what to look for.

**Before you sign:** ask how the deposit is held, what the landlord treats as normal wear and tear, and what their cleaning expectations are at move-out. These are reasonable [questions to ask when touring](https://www.brightplace.ai/resources/questions-to-ask-when-touring-an-apartment), and the answers are easier to get before you are a tenant.

**At move-out:** clean thoroughly, photograph everything again, attend the walkthrough if one is offered, and hand over your forwarding address in writing.

**Keep every record** until the deposit is settled: the lease, receipts, your written address, and any correspondence. If someone [cosigned or guaranteed your lease](https://www.brightplace.ai/resources/what-is-a-guarantor-on-a-lease), tell them where the deposit stands, since their liability does not end until the account is closed.

Budgeting for the deposit alongside first month's rent and fees is part of understanding [your true monthly cost](https://www.brightplace.ai/guides/your-true-monthly-cost). ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a landlord have to return a security deposit?
Most states require return within 14 to 30 days of move-out (as of Q3 2026). New York requires 14 days, California 21, and Texas, Ohio, Washington, Massachusetts, North Carolina and Michigan all use 30. Arizona uses 14 business days, which is longer than it sounds.

### What happens if my landlord never returns my security deposit?
Send a written demand by certified mail, then file in small claims court if that fails. Most states let you recover more than the deposit: twice the amount in New York, California, Arizona, Michigan and Washington, and three times in Texas and Massachusetts for bad-faith retention.

### Does the deadline start when my lease ends or when I move out?
Usually when you move out, not when the lease expires. Texas counts from surrender of the premises. Washington and Ohio require the tenancy to have ended and the unit to be vacant. Michigan counts from the date the landlord receives your written forwarding address.

### Do I have to give my landlord a forwarding address?
In several states, yes, and it must be in writing. Ohio tenants who fail to provide one are not entitled to damages or attorney's fees at all. Texas landlords need not refund until they receive one. Michigan requires it within four days of moving out.

### Can a landlord charge me for carpet cleaning?
Only if the carpet has damage beyond normal wear and tear, such as stains or rips. Ordinary wear along walking paths is not chargeable. If the carpet was already part-way through its useful life when you moved in, full replacement cost is not a reasonable deduction.

### Can a landlord charge me for repainting?
Generally not for ordinary fading or minor scuffs, which are normal wear and tear. Painting becomes chargeable when there is damage beyond ordinary use, such as large holes, smoke damage or writing on the walls. Paint also depreciates, so full repainting costs are rarely justified.

### What is normal wear and tear?
Normal wear and tear is the decline in a rental's condition from ordinary use by a tenant who cleaned and maintained it. Faded paint, worn carpet traffic lanes and small nail holes qualify. Holes in drywall, pet damage and staining do not, because they exceed ordinary use.

### Does my landlord have to give me receipts for deductions?
It depends on the state. California requires invoices or receipts for any deduction over $125. Massachusetts requires a sworn itemized list with written evidence attached. Other states require only an itemized statement, though a list with no documentation is weak evidence in court.

### Is it worth suing over a small security deposit?
Often yes. Small claims filing fees are modest and no lawyer is needed. Because many states award two or three times the amount wrongfully withheld, an $800 deposit can support a claim worth $1,600 to $2,400. Calculate the statutory figure before deciding.

### How much can a landlord charge for a security deposit?
Caps vary. New York limits deposits to one month's rent. Arizona and Michigan cap them at one and a half months' rent. Massachusetts limits them to the first month's rent. Several states set no statutory cap, leaving the amount to the lease agreement.

### What if my landlord misses the deadline but I did cause damage?
In several states a landlord who misses the deadline forfeits the right to keep any of the deposit, including for genuine damage. The deadline is a condition of the right to deduct, not just a target. Check your state's statute for how strictly it applies.

### Can I use my security deposit as last month's rent?
Usually not, unless the lease says so. A security deposit and prepaid rent are separate categories of money, in the same way [prorated rent](https://www.brightplace.ai/resources/prorated-rent) is calculated separately at move-in. Withholding your final rent payment typically gives the landlord a valid deduction and can put you in breach, which makes recovering anything harder.
