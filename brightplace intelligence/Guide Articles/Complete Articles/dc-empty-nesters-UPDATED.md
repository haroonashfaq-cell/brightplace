# Empty Nester Apartments in the DC Suburbs: Where to Downsize and What It Costs

Empty nester apartments in the DC suburbs range from $2,200 to $3,500 per month for a two-bedroom unit (as of Q3 2026). Bethesda, Arlington, Old Town Alexandria, and North Bethesda each offer walkable cores with Metro access, healthcare proximity, and maintenance-free apartment living for downsizers leaving single-family homes in the DC metro.

## Why Are Empty Nesters in the DC Metro Switching to Apartments?

The financial case for downsizing from a house to an apartment in the DC suburbs has strengthened every year since 2020. Homeowners in [Montgomery County](https://www.montgomerycountymd.gov) pay a median property tax bill of $7,500 to $9,000 per year (as of Q3 2026) on a $600,000 home. Add annual maintenance costs of 1% to 4% of the home's value, homeowners insurance, and utilities for a four-bedroom house, and the true cost of ownership runs well above comparable apartment rent.

Empty nesters who sell a $900,000 to $1.2 million home in the DC suburbs free up significant equity while eliminating property tax, maintenance, and repair obligations entirely. A two-bedroom apartment in these same walkable suburbs costs $2,800 to $3,500 per month (as of Q3 2026), which often represents a net savings when the full cost of homeownership is factored in.

The lifestyle shift matters too. No more coordinating contractors for roof repairs, no more weekend yard work, no more managing a house that's 1,500 square feet larger than two people need. Apartment living in the DC metro's walkable cores means walking to restaurants, groceries, and Metro stations instead of driving everywhere.

## What Does Rent Look Like Across the DC Suburbs?

Rent varies significantly by suburb and building type. Here is what empty nesters should expect to budget across four of the strongest neighborhoods for downsizers (as of Q3 2026):

**Bethesda, MD:** One-bedroom apartments average $2,300 to $2,750 per month (as of Q3 2026). Two-bedroom units run $3,300 to $4,000 per month. Studios start around $1,828 per month. Bethesda carries the highest rents on this list but also offers the most walkable downtown core.

**Arlington, VA:** One-bedroom apartments average $2,400 to $2,585 per month (as of Q3 2026). Two-bedroom units cost $2,960 to $3,550 per month. Rents have increased about 3% year-over-year in the first half of 2026.

**Old Town Alexandria, VA:** One-bedroom apartments average $2,200 to $2,750 per month (as of Q3 2026). Two-bedroom units run $2,800 to $3,500 per month. Older stabilized buildings offer lower rents, while new luxury developments like CityHouse command premiums.

**North Bethesda, MD:** One-bedroom apartments average $2,186 to $2,393 per month (as of Q3 2026). Two-bedroom units cost $2,700 to $2,898 per month. This is the most affordable entry point among the four neighborhoods, with rents trending slightly down in 2026.

North Bethesda and Old Town Alexandria represent the best value for empty nesters on a defined budget. Bethesda and the Arlington corridor cost more but deliver the highest walkability and the shortest commute times to downtown DC.

## How Do You Choose Between Maryland and Virginia Suburbs?

The Maryland versus Virginia decision comes down to taxes, lifestyle, and proximity to family or work. Most empty nesters overlook the income tax difference, and it is substantial.

**Virginia income tax:** The top rate is 5.75% on income above $17,001 (as of Q3 2026). Virginia has no local or county income tax. Social Security income is not taxed. Retirement income up to $12,000 is deductible.

**Maryland income tax (Montgomery County):** The state top rate is 6.5%, and Montgomery County adds a 3.20% local income tax (as of Q3 2026). The combined top marginal rate is 9.70%.

At $100,000 of annual income, a renter in Montgomery County, Maryland pays approximately $2,600 more per year in income taxes than a renter in Arlington, Virginia (as of Q3 2026). That $2,600 annual difference covers roughly one month of the rent gap between Arlington and cheaper suburbs. No competing guide for empty nesters mentions this.

Maryland does offer advantages. Montgomery County's public library system, Bethesda's restaurant scene, and the coming Purple Line (Bethesda to New Carrollton, opening December 2027) add real infrastructure value. The decision is not straightforward, and renters can test a neighborhood for a year before committing long-term.

## What Makes Bethesda a Strong Fit for Empty Nesters?

Bethesda is one of the most complete walkable suburban environments in the DC metro. Bethesda Row offers independent restaurants, a movie theater, boutiques, and a Whole Foods within a few blocks of most apartment buildings. The Capital Crescent Trail runs from Georgetown through Bethesda to Silver Spring, giving residents a paved, tree-lined fitness corridor that does not require a car. The [Metro Red Line](https://www.wmata.com) station puts downtown DC about 20 minutes away (as of Q3 2026).

For empty nesters specifically, Bethesda works because the community skews older and more established than Arlington's Rosslyn-Ballston corridor. Evenings are calm. Restaurants are worth lingering in. NIH, Walter Reed National Military Medical Center, and Suburban Hospital (a Johns Hopkins affiliate) are all within minutes, making healthcare access among the strongest in the entire metro area.

The tradeoff is cost. Bethesda commands the highest rents on this list. A two-bedroom apartment near Bethesda Row runs $3,300 to $4,000 per month (as of Q3 2026). Empty nesters who prioritize walkability and cultural infrastructure over price will find Bethesda difficult to beat.

**Worth looking at:** 7001 Arlington at Bethesda (7001 Arlington Rd, Bethesda, MD 20814). Managed by Bozzuto. One-bedrooms from approximately $2,445 per month; two-bedrooms from approximately $3,599 per month (as of Q3 2026). Steps from Bethesda Row, direct Capital Crescent Trail access, dedicated green space, and Bethesda Metro under a 10-minute walk.

## Is Arlington a Good Alternative for Downsizers?

Arlington's appeal for empty nester apartments in the DC suburbs centers on transit density and tax savings. The Rosslyn-Ballston corridor has five Metro stations (Orange and Silver Lines) within two miles, and Pentagon City adds Blue and Yellow Line access. Reagan National Airport sits minutes away, which matters when adult children are scattered across the country.

Pentagon City and Crystal City have evolved significantly since Amazon HQ2's arrival. New retail, dining, and the Potomac waterfront trail system give outdoor-oriented empty nesters real options without needing a car. The neighborhood mix of established residents and younger professionals creates a community that does not feel like a starter apartment complex.

[Arlington County](https://www.arlingtonva.us) also delivers the Virginia income tax advantage described above. An empty nester earning $100,000 saves roughly $2,600 per year compared to Montgomery County (as of Q3 2026). Over a five-year lease, that is $13,000 in tax savings alone.

**Worth looking at:** MAA National Landing (1201 S Joyce St, Arlington, VA 22202). Indoor heated pool, rooftop terrace, fireplaces in select units, and a guest apartment for visiting family. No breed or weight restrictions on pets.

Pentagon City Metro is within walking distance. One-bedrooms start at approximately $2,010 per month (as of Q3 2026).

## What Is It Like to Rent in Old Town Alexandria?

Old Town Alexandria is the DC metro's most fully realized walkable small city. The waterfront runs for miles. King Street is lined with independent restaurants, wine bars, coffee shops, and galleries that have been there for decades.

The architecture is Federal and Colonial, giving the neighborhood a character that newer developments cannot replicate. The Yellow and Blue Metro lines at King St-Old Town station put everything inside DC a direct ride away (as of Q3 2026).

Empty nesters consistently cite Old Town as one of the most livable environments in the region once the school-district calculation stops mattering. The pressure to be in a particular zip code for school quality lifts, and what remains is one of the most pleasant streetscapes in the mid-Atlantic. Weekend mornings involve a walk to the farmers market and coffee, not a drive to three different activities.

Old Town's rent spread is wider than other suburbs on this list. Older stabilized buildings offer one-bedrooms around $2,200 per month (as of Q3 2026), while new luxury developments like CityHouse (a 199-unit office-to-residential conversion that began leasing in 2026) command significantly higher rents. The 2026 average across all building types sits around $2,800 per month for a two-bedroom.

Inova Alexandria Hospital, recognized as one of America's 100 Best Hospitals, is a short drive from the Old Town core. The free King Street Trolley connects the Metro station to the waterfront, reducing the need for a car even further.

Parking in Old Town is the one consistent tradeoff. Street parking is limited and permit-based. Building garages cost extra, typically $150 to $250 per month (as of Q3 2026). Empty nesters who can go car-light or car-free will save meaningfully here.

## Should You Consider North Bethesda for Lower Rent?

North Bethesda sits just above Bethesda on the Red Line and offers a quieter, lower-density alternative with the same Metro access at a lower price point. The Pike and Rose development, a LEED-certified mixed-use district covering 9 walkable blocks, anchors the neighborhood with a cinema, restaurants, a gym, green space, and apartment living all connected to the White Flint Metro station.

For empty nesters who want to stay in Montgomery County but want more space and less cost than downtown Bethesda, North Bethesda threads the needle. A two-bedroom apartment averages $2,700 to $2,898 per month (as of Q3 2026), saving $500 to $1,000 per month compared to equivalent Bethesda units.

The Strathmore campus (the arts center, the mansion, and the surrounding grounds) gives North Bethesda a genuine cultural anchor within walking distance. For empty nesters who spent years driving kids to activities, having world-class chamber music and visual art a short walk from home is a meaningful quality-of-life addition.

Looking forward, the Purple Line will terminate at Bethesda with improved connections through Silver Spring and beyond when it opens in December 2027 (as of Q3 2026). This will significantly improve Montgomery County's east-west transit, making North Bethesda even better connected for residents who want to explore without a car.

## What Does the Rent-Versus-Own Math Actually Look Like?

The financial case for selling a house and renting an apartment depends on your specific equity, mortgage status, and tax situation. Here is a worked example for a typical DC-area empty nester (as of Q3 2026):

**Selling a $1,000,000 home in Montgomery County:**

**Annual costs eliminated:** Property tax of $11,900 per year. Home insurance of $2,400 per year. Maintenance at 2% of value, $20,000 per year. Total eliminated: approximately $34,300 per year (as of Q3 2026).

**Renting a two-bedroom in North Bethesda at $2,800 per month:** Annual rent cost of $33,600. Renter's insurance of $200 per year. Total new cost: approximately $33,800 per year (as of Q3 2026).

In this scenario, the annual housing cost is essentially the same, but the empty nester now holds the proceeds from a home sale (potentially $500,000 to $800,000 in equity after paying off the mortgage (as of Q3 2026)). That equity can be invested, used for travel, or reserved as a financial cushion. The true cost comparison favors renting even more when you factor in the opportunity cost of capital locked in home equity.

## What Should Empty Nesters Know Before Signing a Lease?

Moving from a house you have owned for 15 to 25 years into an apartment involves more than just packing. Here are the practical realities that catch most downsizers off guard:

**Storage is the first challenge.** A 2,800-square-foot house does not compress into a 1,100-square-foot apartment without significant decluttering. Start sorting six months before your move date. Donate or sell furniture that will not fit the new layout. Keep items with genuine daily utility and let go of pieces that only made sense in a larger space.

**Noise is different.** Apartment walls are thinner than house walls. Ask about the building's construction type during tours. Concrete and steel buildings are significantly quieter than wood-frame construction. Top-floor and corner units reduce neighbor noise on multiple sides.

**Lease flexibility matters.** Most DC-area apartments offer 12-month leases, but some communities allow [month-to-month agreements](https://www.brightplace.ai/resources/month-to-month-vs-12-month-lease) after the initial term. For empty nesters testing a neighborhood before committing, this flexibility is valuable.

**Rent increases are real.** Virginia has no statewide rent control (as of Q3 2026). Montgomery County has limited rent stabilization for certain older buildings. DC proper has rent control for buildings built before 1975. Build a 3% to 5% annual rent increase assumption into your long-term budget.

**Pet policies vary.** If you have a pet, confirm breed restrictions, weight limits, and monthly pet rent before applying. Some buildings charge $25 to $75 per month in [pet fees](https://www.brightplace.ai/resources/pet-deposit-vs-pet-fee) on top of rent (as of Q3 2026).

Renters in any of these markets are protected under [federal fair housing guidelines](https://www.brightplace.ai/resources/fair-housing-act-guidelines), which prohibit discrimination based on age, disability, familial status, and other protected categories. The [U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development](https://hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp) provides resources for renters who encounter discrimination.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Empty Nester Apartments in the DC Suburbs

### What is the average rent for a two-bedroom apartment in the DC suburbs?

Two-bedroom apartment rents across DC's walkable suburbs range from $2,700 to $4,000 per month (as of Q3 2026). North Bethesda offers the lowest entry point at $2,700 to $2,898 per month, while downtown Bethesda commands the highest rents at $3,300 to $4,000 per month. Arlington and Old Town Alexandria fall between these ranges.

### Is it cheaper to rent in Maryland or Virginia suburbs of DC?

Monthly rent is similar across Maryland and Virginia suburbs of DC, but income taxes differ significantly (as of Q3 2026). Virginia charges a top rate of 5.75% with no local income tax. Montgomery County, Maryland combines a 6.5% state rate with a 3.20% county tax. At $100,000 income, Virginia residents save approximately $2,600 per year.

### Can you live without a car in Bethesda or Arlington?

Both Bethesda and Arlington support car-light or car-free living for residents near Metro stations. Bethesda's downtown core has grocery stores, restaurants, and medical facilities within walking distance of the Red Line. Arlington's Rosslyn-Ballston corridor has five Metro stations within two miles. Many empty nesters drop to one car or none after moving.

### What size apartment do empty nesters typically need?

Most empty nesters in the DC suburbs rent one-bedroom or two-bedroom apartments (as of Q3 2026). A two-bedroom provides a dedicated guest room for visiting adult children and a home office. Units range from 780 to 1,200 square feet. Couples who entertain frequently or work from home generally prefer two-bedrooms.

### Is downsizing from a house to an apartment worth it financially?

For most DC-area homeowners, downsizing eliminates $25,000 to $40,000 per year in property taxes, maintenance, and insurance (as of Q3 2026). Renting a comparable-quality two-bedroom apartment costs $33,000 to $42,000 per year. The net cost difference is small, but downsizers also free up home equity for investment or retirement.

### What hospitals are near Bethesda and Arlington?

Bethesda sits adjacent to NIH Clinical Center, Walter Reed National Military Medical Center, and Suburban Hospital (a Johns Hopkins affiliate). Arlington has VHC Health (formerly Virginia Hospital Center). Old Town Alexandria has Inova Alexandria Hospital, recognized among America's 100 Best Hospitals. Healthcare access is a major advantage across all four neighborhoods.

### How far are these suburbs from downtown DC by Metro?

Bethesda to downtown DC takes approximately 20 minutes on the Red Line (as of Q3 2026). Arlington's Pentagon City and Rosslyn stations reach downtown in 10 to 15 minutes. Old Town Alexandria's King St station takes 20 to 30 minutes on the Blue or Yellow Line. North Bethesda reaches downtown in 25 to 35 minutes on the Red Line.

### What is the Purple Line and how will it affect these neighborhoods?

The Purple Line is a 16-mile light rail line connecting Bethesda to New Carrollton in Prince George's County, Maryland (as of Q3 2026). It is scheduled to open in December 2027. The line will significantly improve east-west transit in Montgomery County, making Bethesda and Silver Spring more connected without requiring a car or a transfer through downtown DC.

### Do DC-area apartments offer month-to-month leases for downsizers?

Most apartment communities in the DC suburbs require an initial 12-month lease but offer month-to-month renewal options afterward (as of Q3 2026). Monthly rates on month-to-month terms are typically 10% to 15% higher than the locked annual rate. This flexibility lets empty nesters test a neighborhood before committing long-term.

### What should empty nesters look for when touring apartments?

Empty nesters should prioritize building construction type (concrete over wood-frame for noise reduction), elevator access, in-unit laundry, and proximity to Metro and grocery stores. Ask about guest parking policies for visiting family, storage unit availability, and whether the building has a package room. Check the lease terms for rent increase caps and early termination options.

### Are pets allowed in most DC-area apartments?

Most apartment communities in the DC suburbs allow pets, but policies vary by building (as of Q3 2026). Common restrictions include breed-specific bans, weight limits of 50 to 75 pounds, and one to two pet maximums. Monthly pet rent ranges from $25 to $75 per pet. Some buildings like MAA National Landing have no breed or weight restrictions.
