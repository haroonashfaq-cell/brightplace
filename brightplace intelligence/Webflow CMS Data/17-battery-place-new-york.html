What Is 17 Battery Place?

17 Battery Place is the Whitehall Building, an NYC-landmarked 31-story mixed-use property at the corner of Battery Place and Washington Street in Manhattan's Financial District. Built in 1904 and designated a city landmark in 2000, the building's upper floors currently operate as rental apartments while its lower floors remain office space. A second conversion phase, announced in April 2026, will add 220 residential units by Q1 2027, including 55 permanently affordable apartments. For renters researching the Financial District, this building represents one of Lower Manhattan's most distinctive addresses.

The Whitehall Building: A Brief History Worth Knowing

Architect Henry Hardenberge, who also designed the Dakota and the Plaza Hotel, completed the original Whitehall Building in 1904. Developer Robert Chesebrough, the inventor of Vaseline, funded the project. The NYC Landmarks Preservation Commission designated the Whitehall Building as an official city landmark on October 17, 2000 (designated October 2000).

The Renaissance Revival facade, which uses classical proportions and ornamental detailing drawn from Italian Renaissance buildings, features rusticated limestone, terracotta cladding, and multi-tone brick. The building's exterior restoration, completed in 2024, replaced more than 3,000 ornamental terracotta pieces. Before its residential life, the building served as a harbor dispatch center for tugboat companies. Operators on the upper floors used megaphones to direct captains at the docks below. That detail rarely appears in listing pages, but it connects the building's waterfront identity to the views current residents see from the same windows.

Who Lives at 17 Battery Place Today

The building's upper floors (14 through 31) were first converted to rental apartments in the early 2000s under the Moinian Group, delivering approximately 500 existing units. A mixed-use building combines residential apartments and commercial space within the same structure; at 17 Battery Place, floors 2 through 13 remain office space while upper floors operate as rental apartments. The building is also listed as 1 West Street for residential purposes.

The Moinian Group's second conversion phase will add 220 residential units, including 55 permanently affordable units, with completion targeted for Q1 2027 (announced April 2026). New York State's 467-m program, enacted in 2024, provides tax incentives to developers who include permanently affordable residential units in office-to-residential conversions. The 55 affordable units at 17 Battery Place are a direct result of this program. On-site amenities confirmed by the developer include a rooftop deck, a Lifetime Fitness studio, and a landscaped outdoor plaza. 17 Battery Place contains approximately 940,000 total square feet across its combined North and South structures (as of 2024).

Honest Tradeoffs: What to Consider Before Renting Here

Here is what the listings do not tell you.

The Financial District is quieter than most Manhattan neighborhoods on evenings and weekends, especially south of Fulton Street. The daytime population is predominantly office workers, and the sidewalks thin out after 7 PM. Some renters prefer this calm. Others find it isolating.

Grocery and everyday errands require planning. The nearest full-service supermarket options are a meaningful walk north. Convenience stores cover basics, but weekly shopping takes more effort here than in Midtown or the Upper West Side.
