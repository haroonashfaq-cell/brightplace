Apartments with no credit check do exist, but they require a different search strategy than browsing major listing sites. Most no-credit-check rentals come from private landlords managing a small number of units, not large apartment complexes. This guide covers how to rent an apartment with no credit, what landlords accept instead of a credit score, and how to spot scams in the process.

Do Apartments With No Credit Check Actually Exist?

Apartments with no credit check do exist, primarily through private landlords who own a small number of units. Large apartment complexes almost always run credit screens with minimum score thresholds of 620 to 670 (as of Q3 2026). Private landlords are far more likely to skip the pull entirely or accept alternative proof of financial reliability.

Understanding the type of inquiry matters. A soft pull shows your credit history but does not affect your score. A hard pull can lower your score by a few points and stays on your credit report for up to two years. If a landlord advertises "no credit check," confirm whether they mean no pull at all or a soft pull.

Who Is Most Likely to Skip the Credit Check

Small-scale landlords and informal rental arrangements are the most likely to skip credit screening entirely. The probability depends on the rental provider type.

Large property management: Yes, almost always requires a credit check. These companies use automated screening platforms that pull credit, criminal history, and eviction records as a package.

Small private landlord (1-4 units): Often no, or flexible. Owners managing a handful of units rely on income verification and references rather than formal credit reports.

Sublet/roommate: Rarely requires a credit check. The original tenant makes the decision informally. See brightplace's guide to renters insurance with roommates for shared-living considerations.

Income-based/Section 8: Screening differs, income-focused. HUD-assisted housing uses income eligibility as the primary qualifier, and low credit scores are less likely to cause denial. How to Find No-Credit-Check Apartments

The most reliable channels for finding apartments without credit checks are local, informal, and offline.

Facebook Marketplace and local city groups. Search "[your city] rentals" in Facebook Groups. Private landlords post here because it is free and reaches local renters directly.

Craigslist housing section. Look for listings posted by owner rather than by agent. Small landlords list here without screening integrations.

Driving for "For Rent" signs. Physical signs on duplexes, triplexes, and single-family homes indicate an owner managing the property directly.

Local housing authorities. Contact your county or city housing authority about income-based programs. The HUD rental assistance page lists local agencies by state.

Asking directly. Call and ask about the screening process before paying an application fee. For a full walkthrough, see brightplace's guide to renting an apartment.

What to Offer Instead of a Credit Score

Landlords who skip credit checks still need assurance you can pay rent reliably. Five substitutes carry real weight.

1. Larger security deposit (2-3 months). Offering two to three months' rent as a deposit reduces the landlord's risk. Check your state's deposit cap laws first. See brightplace's guide to homes for rent with no deposit for more.

2. Prepaid rent (3-6 months). Paying several months upfront demonstrates financial stability. Get the prepayment terms written into your lease.

3. Co-signer or guarantor. A co-signer shares legal responsibility for the lease alongside you. A guarantor is financially responsible only if you default. In NYC, the standard is income of 40 times the monthly rent, or a guarantor earning 27.5 times the annual rent (as of Q3 2026). See brightplace's guide to sublet apartments in NYC for alternative paths in.

4. Proof of income (3x rent). Pay stubs, bank statements, or an employment letter showing income of at least three times the monthly rent is the most common substitute. See brightplace's guide to your true monthly cost for a full breakdown.

5. Reference letters. Written references from previous landlords confirming on-time payment and lease compliance can substitute for a credit history.

The strongest applications combine two or more of these substitutes.

What the Application Process Looks Like Without a Credit Check

Applying to a no-credit-check landlord is typically faster and less formal than the process at a large apartment complex, but you still need to come prepared. Most private landlords who skip credit screening make their decision based on documents you bring to the showing or submit within 24 hours of viewing the unit.

What to bring to the application. Have three to six months of pay stubs or bank statements showing consistent income, a valid photo ID, an employment verification letter from your employer, and contact information for one to two previous landlords who can confirm your rental history. If you are self-employed, bring tax returns or 1099 forms from the most recent filing year. Some renters have found that presenting a one-page "renter resume" summarizing their income, rental history, and references makes a strong first impression with private landlords.

Application fees are lower with private landlords. Expect to pay $0 to $50 for an application with a private landlord (as of Q3 2026), compared to $50 to $100 at large apartment complexes that run full screening packages. Some private landlords charge no application fee at all.

Same-day approval is common. Because private landlords are making a personal judgment rather than waiting for automated screening results, many offer approval within the same day. Bring all your documents to the showing so the landlord can review everything in one meeting. Lease flexibility may be available. Private landlords are often willing to negotiate lease length, especially if you offer a larger deposit or prepaid rent. If you are unsure about committing to a full year, ask about shorter terms. brightplace's guide to month-to-month vs. 12-month leases breaks down the cost and flexibility tradeoffs for each option.

Third-Party Guarantor Services

Third-party guarantor services act as a professional co-signer for renters who lack the credit history or income to qualify on their own. These companies typically charge 4 to 10 percent of the annual rent (as of Q3 2026) and are most relevant in high-demand markets like NYC, LA, and San Francisco. If you do not have a personal guarantor, a third-party service can fill the gap.

What "No Credit Check" Can Actually Mean (And When to Be Cautious)

Legitimate no-credit-check rentals exist, but the phrase also attracts scammers. The FTC's guide to renting a home outlines common fraud tactics. Four red flags should prompt you to walk away.

Deposit demanded before seeing the unit. No legitimate landlord requires payment before you visit the property.

Stock photos in the listing. Images that appear on multiple listings across different addresses indicate fraud.

Wire money or gift card requests. Legitimate landlords accept checks, money orders, or standard payment platforms.

Rent far below market rate. A listing priced 30 to 50 percent below comparable units is suspicious until verified in person.

Legitimate landlords meet you in person, show the unit, and provide a written lease. For more on credit challenges, see brightplace's guide on how to get an apartment with bad credit.

Can You Still Build Credit While Renting Without a Check?

Yes. Rent-reporting services allow you to report monthly rent payments to one or more credit bureaus, building payment history even if your landlord does not report directly. This is one of the most practical ways to turn a no-credit-check rental into a stepping stone toward stronger future applications.

How rent reporting works. You enroll with a rent-reporting service and either connect your bank account or submit proof of each monthly payment. The service then reports your on-time payments to one, two, or all three major credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion). Not all services report to all three bureaus, so check which bureaus a service covers before enrolling.

What it costs. Most rent-reporting services charge $5 to $10 per month (as of Q3 2026). Some landlords and property management companies now report rent payments directly to credit bureaus at no cost to the tenant, though this is more common at larger complexes than with private landlords.

What to expect. Twelve months of consistent on-time rent payments can add 20 to 40 points to a thin credit file, according to industry data. The impact is strongest for renters with little or no existing credit history. Confirm your payments appear on your report through AnnualCreditReport.com after two to three months of reporting.

Building credit through rent reporting makes your next lease application significantly easier, potentially qualifying you for apartments at larger complexes with lower deposits. Frequently Asked Questions

Are there any apartments that don't require a credit check?

Yes. Private landlords managing one to four units are the most common source. Sublets, roommate arrangements, and some income-based housing programs also skip credit screening. Large complexes managed by property management companies almost always require one (as of Q3 2026).

Can you get a rental without a credit check?

You can. Focus on private landlords, local Facebook groups, Craigslist housing listings, and physical "For Rent" signs. Offer alternatives like a larger deposit, prepaid rent, or a co-signer. Asking about the screening process before applying saves time and fees.

What credit score do you typically need to rent an apartment?

Most large complexes require a minimum between 620 and 670 (as of Q3 2026). Luxury properties may require 700 or higher. Private landlords often have no minimum at all. Strong income, clean rental history, and a larger deposit can offset a lower score.

Is no credit worse than bad credit for renting?

Generally, no. No credit means no negative marks, which many landlords view as less risky than a history of missed payments. You can build a case with income documentation and landlord references more easily when there are no delinquencies on your report.

Can I rent an apartment with a 500 credit score?

Yes, but options at large complexes will be limited. Private landlords, second chance apartments, and income-based housing are realistic paths. A short-term lease can help you build a positive rental record for future applications.

What do private landlords look for instead of a credit score?

Most prioritize proof of income at three times the monthly rent, references from previous landlords confirming on-time payment, employment stability, and a clean eviction history. Offering a larger deposit or several months of prepaid rent strengthens your application further.

How do I rent an apartment with no credit history?

Start by targeting private landlords who manage one to four units, as they are the most likely to skip credit screening. Search local Facebook groups, Craigslist housing sections, and look for physical "For Rent" signs on smaller properties. Prepare alternatives like proof of income at three times the rent, a larger security deposit, or a co-signer. Asking about the screening process before applying saves time and application fees.

Are there low income apartments with no credit check?

Yes. Income-based housing programs, including HUD-assisted and Section 8 properties, use income eligibility as the primary qualifier rather than credit scores. Contact your local housing authority or visit the HUD rental assistance page to find income-based programs in your area. Some private landlords in lower-cost markets also skip credit checks entirely if you can demonstrate stable income.
