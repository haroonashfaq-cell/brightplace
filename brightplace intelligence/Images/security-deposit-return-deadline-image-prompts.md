## Visual Identity Brief

1. **Subject type:** Conceptual interior still life. Not a property, not a neighborhood.
   The article is national and legal, so any recognisable building or region would
   mislead.
2. **Building style:** N/A. Generic contemporary rental interior.
3. **Materials and colors:** Warm neutrals. Pale wall, light oak or white-painted sill,
   brushed metal keys, cream paper.
4. **Architectural details:** A window frame and sill. Soft daylight as the only light
   source.
5. **Landscape:** None. Interior only.
6. **Amenities visible:** None.
7. **Geographic setting:** Deliberately neutral. No palms, no snow, no skyline. The
   article covers ten states and must not look like any one of them.
8. **Season:** Early autumn light, matching a September publication. Low warm sun.
9. **What renters describe:** The move-out moment. Threads about deposits center on the
   day the keys go back and the unit is empty. That handover is the emotional center.
10. **The #1 visual hook:** **Keys resting on a lease document.** It reads instantly as
    the end of a tenancy and the moment the deposit clock starts, which is literally
    the article's subject.

**Fair Housing:** no people visible, no indicator of who lives there.

---

## Prompt Option A — RECOMMENDED

> A warm editorial photograph of a small set of apartment keys on a brass ring resting
> on a folded paper lease document, placed on a pale wooden windowsill. Soft early
> autumn morning daylight comes through the window from the left, casting a long gentle
> shadow across the paper. Shallow depth of field, the keys in sharp focus and the
> window softly blurred behind. Muted warm neutral palette: cream paper, pale oak,
> brushed metal. Clean minimal composition with generous negative space on the right.
> Natural light photography, editorial magazine style, no text, no logos, no people,
> no signage. Shot on a 50mm lens at f/2.0.

**Why this one:** the keys-on-lease pairing is the single clearest visual for "tenancy
ended, deposit clock running." Negative space on the right leaves room for a title
overlay if the design ever needs one.

---

## Prompt Option B

> A warm editorial photograph of an empty rental apartment living room on move-out day,
> completely bare, with clean pale walls and light wood flooring. Afternoon sunlight
> falls across the floor in soft geometric shapes from an uncovered window. A few faint
> rectangular marks on the wall where pictures once hung. No furniture, no boxes, no
> people. Muted warm neutral palette, natural light photography, editorial style, no
> text, no logos. Wide angle, 28mm.

**Why not first:** the faint picture marks are a nice nod to normal wear and tear, but
an empty room reads as "vacancy" more than "deposit," which is a different article.

---

## Prompt Option C

> A warm editorial still life of a plain cream envelope and a folded document on a light
> kitchen counter beside a single cardboard moving box, lit by soft window daylight from
> the right. Shallow depth of field. Muted warm neutral palette, clean minimal
> composition, natural light photography, editorial magazine style, no text, no logos,
> no people, no visible writing on the envelope. 50mm lens.

**Why not first:** the envelope implies the refund arriving, which is the ending rather
than the question. Weaker match to a headline that asks "how long."
